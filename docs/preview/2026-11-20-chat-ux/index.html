1 · The sidebar's three views

The three dots sit in the bottom row, in fixed order: Recent, Folders, General. The active name shows beside them. Firstmate's card and the dots each keep their own space, and the list between them scrolls as far as the window allows, with no row cap. Switching views never changes the open chat, where you are in it, or its draft.

  • Recent: every chat, newest by your own last message. Pinned chats come first, in your order, with a pin mark rather than a heading. Each row's small grey label is its folder; clicking it switches to Folders and shows the chat under it.
  • Folders: folder names only, newest by your last message in each. A dot means unread replies inside; the pencil means an unsent draft behind its +. The chat you opened during this visit stays under its folder.
  • General: chats with no folder. A chat filed into a folder leaves General but stays in Recent.
  • Rows: a bold title is an unread reply. Three small dots mean Firstmate is writing in that chat right now. The ••• menu and + appear on hover and keyboard focus.
  • New chat and Search chats sit side by side under the tiles. The existing Search tile (⌘K) stays as it is.

2 · A folder: hover, choose, expand

Like Arc's folders in your pictures. Hovering a folder opens a panel beside the sidebar: + New chat at the top, then every chat in it, newest first, scrolling inside the panel. The pointer can move into it. Choosing a chat closes the panel and leaves that chat under the folder. Clicking the folder's own name lists all of its chats in the sidebar instead.

  • Keyboard: Tab reaches the folder; → or Enter opens the same panel with the first chat focused; ↑↓ move; Enter opens; Esc closes and returns focus to the folder. Space expands the folder inline.
  • The panel is 250 pixels wide and never taller than the window allows (the 24-chat Gitex here scrolls). It never covers Firstmate's card or the dots.

3 · Home

Home keeps its overview and Needs your attention. One line is added at the top: Continue, naming the chat you last opened, even if you only read it. Selecting it takes you back to where you stopped. The message box keeps unsent words across screens and restarts; the same draft appears in New chat.

4 · Firstmate's card (the Island)

Decisions come first, then replies. A decision shows the project and request, the actual question, the choices with the recommended one marked, and Open request. Answering never leaves the chat you are in. One unread reply gets Open reply; several become a count that opens a short list.

  • Waiting to send: if the connection drops after you choose, your choice stays on the card until Amun checks with Firstmate. It is sent once, only if the question is unchanged. If the question changed, the new choices show. If it was settled elsewhere, the recorded answer shows.
  • No global "Waiting for Firstmate". A busy crew never blocks another chat.

5 · Ask about a card

"Ask about Gitex" on the project panel opens a fresh chat in the Gitex folder. A small Gitex · Project attachment sits above the text, and the full width stays free for typing. Clicking the attachment shows exactly what will be sent with your first message. Opening it sends nothing. The folder's own unfinished draft stays behind its +.

6 · Requests and deliveries in a chat

Each request gets one quiet status line under your message, in plain words from Firstmate's reports. Clicking it shows the hand-offs and reports. A finished document arrives as a new Firstmate reply at the bottom, with a link back to the request, and the request line turns to Ready · View reply. The Library rail opens on This chat, with a second view named for the folder.

  • Status words used: Researcher is gathering evidence · Waiting for the research report · Queued for Product Manager (only when Firstmate confirms a capacity wait) · Product Manager is drafting the PRD · Needs your decision · Didn't finish · Ready. Never "Queued for the Gitex team".
  • Library: one entry per document, opening its latest version; earlier versions are one click away. An old delivery reply opens its own version. A document that cannot be read keeps its entry and says why.

7 · Moving a chat that has unfinished requests

Moving a chat and changing a request's project are separate. The second needs Firstmate to agree, so you pick exactly which requests to reassign. None are ticked to begin with.

  • After moving with the email ticked: both requests stay in the moved chat. The PRD carries on for Gitex and its document links to Gitex. The email shows "Change requested" until Firstmate confirms Amun; if Firstmate refuses, or the email finishes first, that outcome shows instead.
  • Splitting from Firstmate's card while you read another chat keeps you where you are and shows "Moved to Gitex · Open chat". The pin stays with the original chat.

8 · Search chats

Results are grouped by chat and labelled with the folder or General. Choosing a passage opens the chat at it with a short highlight; Back to results brings back the same query, matches and position.

9 · Short window, long titles, busy card

At 640 pixels tall with a decision on Firstmate's card, the list still scrolls between the top and the card; nothing overlaps. Long titles are cut with an ellipsis on one line, and the full title shows on hover.

What Firstmate can tell Amun today

Sections 4, 6 and 7 depend on Amun knowing which request a later report belongs to. I checked the code: today nothing links them. Amun sends Firstmate your words plus the folder and chat as plain text, and keeps no id for the message. Firstmate's own ids (task ids, backlog items, decision keys, the secondmate corr= reference) never point back to an Amun message or chat. Replies that come later, such as a finished report, are filed in General, not in the chat that asked.
